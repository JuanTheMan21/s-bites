{# T18B: movie-style captions -- one cue (1-2 lines) on screen at a time, appearing all at once at its own start and clearing the instant the next cue begins, using ``mux/caption_cues.py``'s grouping (shared with the ``.srt`` sidecar so the two can never disagree about where a cue starts or ends). Before this, every word ever shown stayed on screen for the rest of the scene, so captions grew into an unreadable wall of text -- the exact bug this replaces. A cue's words are NOT inked in one at a time. An earlier version kept a per-word karaoke-style reveal inside each cue (each word dimmed until its own real `offset_ms`) -- found, after a real render, to still read as "appearing word by word" even though the wall-of-text bug was fixed; the whole point of a movie-style caption is that a line (or two) appears as one unit. Every word in a cue is at full color from the moment the cue itself becomes visible. captions_markup(prefix, cues, duration_sec) -- one hidden div per cue, each holding its own word spans (kept individually addressable for future styling, not for per-word animation), ids namespaced "{prefix}-cap-{cue index}-{word index}" so two cues' words never collide. captions_script(prefix, cues, duration_sec) -- shows each cue's div, instantly, at its own start; hides it, instantly, the moment the next cue's starts (or, for the last cue, at the segment's own measured duration -- T18K: this was the last cue's own end_ms before, which left a blank band for any trailing silence after the final word). No per-word timing left to get wrong. Sits inside `hyperframes check --caption-zone`'s default guard band (bottom ~18% of the frame) by construction -- pinned to the bottom of #root with a fixed inset. That guard is about the caption band's OWN position, not about content elsewhere on screen growing into it -- see tasks.md's T18C entry for the follow-up this doesn't cover. T18K: the layer carries `data-layout-allow-caption-zone` -- found missing by a real render, not by reasoning about it. Before the same task's `hideAt` fix above, the last cue always hid at its own `end_ms`, safely before `validate_geometry`'s end-of-timeline sample point, so the checker never actually caught a caption word sitting in its own reserved band.
