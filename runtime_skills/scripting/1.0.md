# Writing narration

You are writing the words a synthetic voice will speak over one segment. Target roughly **28
seconds**, which is about **70 to 80 words** at the pace this voice reads. Write to the idea, not
to the word count — a segment that genuinely takes 60 words is better than one padded to 75.

This text is read aloud and never shown. Everything below follows from that.

## Written for the ear

- **No formatting.** No markdown, no bullets, no headings, no parentheses, no bold. The voice
  reads characters; anything visual either gets spoken aloud or silently dropped, and you do not
  get to choose which.
- **No abbreviations the voice will mangle.** Write "for example", not "e.g." Write "versus", not
  "vs". Write "about two hundred milliseconds", not "~200ms".
- **Spell out symbols.** "less than", not "<". A symbol in narration is a coin flip.
- **Short sentences.** Twelve to eighteen words. A listener cannot re-read a clause they lost, so
  a sentence that needs holding in memory to parse is a sentence they will miss the end of.
- **Vary the rhythm.** Three sentences of identical length read as a metronome. Follow a long one
  with a short one.

## Structure of a segment

**Open on the idea.** The first sentence should carry content — a listener decides in two seconds
whether this segment is worth attention, and "Now let's talk about indexes" is two seconds spent
saying nothing.

**Develop it once.** State the idea, then do exactly one of: give an example, explain the
mechanism, or name the consequence. Not all three.

**Land it.** The last sentence should be the one worth remembering. Do not trail off into a
qualifier.

## Segments are independent

Each segment is narrated, measured, and muxed as a self-contained clip. So:

- **No references to other segments.** No "as we saw", no "we will cover this shortly", no "first,
  second, finally" spanning segments. A segment may be cut or reordered and the reference would
  survive it.
- **No pronouns pointing outside the segment.** Open with the noun, not with "it" or "this".
- **Re-establish the subject briefly** if the segment would otherwise be ambiguous standing alone.
  One clause, not a recap.

## Narration and visual

The visual shows the structure; the narration explains it. **Do not read the slide.** If the
visual is a comparison with two labelled columns, the narration says *why the difference matters* —
the labels are already on screen and hearing them read back is the flattest thing an explainer
can do.

Do not describe the visual either. No "as you can see on the left". The viewer can see it, and if
they are listening rather than watching, the sentence is worthless.

## Do not

- Do not write a greeting, a sign-off, or a call to action.
- Do not name the video, the series, or yourself.
- Do not invent numbers, benchmarks, dates, or version numbers to make a sentence land better.
- Do not use rhetorical questions as transitions. Ask a question only when you answer it in the
  next sentence and the answer is surprising.
